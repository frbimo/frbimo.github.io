
# Spiking Neural Network (SNN)

is a type of artificial deep learning for classification using spike as input instead of continuous impulse. So the keyword here: **"signal" is not a continuous value but a discrete spike train over time**


## SNN Simulation
We should simulate SNN to visualize clearly how does it works. We use Leaky Integrate-and-Fire (LIF) neuron which is the most common and computationally efficient model used in large-scale Spiking Neural Networks (SNNs). Why use LIF? because its the simplest model; used for initial conceptual sttudies.

![SNN LIF Animation](/_animations/snn_lif_animation.gif)

The animation above shows how SNNs process information using time and event-based communication—spikes—rather than continuous values.
There are 2 figure sections : **Membrane Potential Line ($V$)** and **Discrete Spike Impulses (Events)**

**Membrane Potential Line ($V$)**: This is the continuous line, usually starting near a low resting potential ($V_{rest}$). It represents the neuron's "readiness" to fire.

**Discrete Spike Impulses (Events)**: These are the sharp, instantaneous vertical lines or dots that appear when the membrane potential reaches a threshold ($V_{thresh}$).

SNN working through two dimensions that distinguish it from standard ANNs: **Time** and **Rate**

 ### :pencil2: The Time Dimension (The "How")

Unlike a traditional ANN, which calculates a single output value, the SNN runs a simulation over time ($T$ or $num\_steps$).


ANN: $Output = \text{Activation}(\text{Weights} \times \text{Input})$
SNN: The decision is spread over many time steps, requiring the model to remember its state ($V$) between steps. The line rising and falling shows this state update across time.

### :pencil2: The Information Coding (The "What")
In the common Rate Coding example used for classification (like the Iris example), the ultimate decision is based on frequency of spikes over the entire simulation window:

- Training/Inference: The SNN runs the simulation, and the output layer's prediction is the neuron that fires the most spikes (highest frequency/rate) during the total simulation time.

- Decay Rate's Role: The rate of decay (leakage) effectively controls how long the neuron "remembers" past input. If decay is fast, the neuron only reacts to instantaneous, strong inputs.


--
**Key Takeaways**:
The only way an SNN neuron can communicate is by overflowing its bucket. A strong, constant input keeps the bucket nearly full, leading to frequent (high rate) spiking. A sparse input allows the bucket to drain significantly between inputs, leading to infrequent (low rate) spiking at a lower average potential.

# Why SNN is cutting edge NN model?
cutting-edge area mentioned implicitly by "edge computing" and "next generation." SNNs are fundamentally different because they mimic the human brain more closely.

- **The Neuron**: Unlike ANNs/RNNs that use continuous values (like a voltage level), SNNs communicate using discrete, binary spikes (like a sudden pulse) when a membrane potential is reached.

- **Core Strength**: Extreme Energy Efficiency. Because the network only performs a calculation when an event (spike) occurs (it's event-driven, or sparse), it is incredibly power efficient. This is ideal for edge computing and neuromorphic hardware.

- **Core Limitation	Maturity & Training**. The training algorithms are less mature, complex, and often result in lower accuracy compared to optimized ANNs/RNNs on standard tasks.
